Verdict on the Original Idea & Benchmarks

Critical reality checks regarding Oracle Cloud Always Free tiers, CPU memory bandwidth bounds, and empirical findings on distributed inference engines.

1. The Verdict Table

Original Claim Rigorous Empirical Finding Confidence
"Merge 10 weak free machines into one AI brain" Oracle Cloud Always Free limits accounts to at most 4 Ampere A1 OCPUs (24GB total) and 2 AMD E2.1.micro nodes. There is no free pool of 10 machines. [V] Verified
"Run heavy 70B models without GPUs on CPU" Feasible for weight storage across RAM, but token generation runs slower than 0.2 tok/s. Memory bandwidth, not capacity, limits interactivity. [E] Estimate
"1 to 5 tokens per second speed" Plausible for 1B to 3B models on fast gigabit LAN or local Docker; tensor-parallel over internet latency drops to <0.1 tok/s. [U] Unknown
"Sequential workers compute the model" Pipeline splitting successfully circumvents intra-layer sync barriers, making it feasible over higher-latency links at the expense of pipeline bubbles. [V] Verified

2. Evaluated Engine Candidates

Supported

llama.cpp RPC Backend

Official ggml-rpc-server backend. Extremely fast on local loops, but highly insecure: transmits unauthenticated raw memory buffers. Must bind strictly to loopback or WireGuard VPN.

Planned M6

distributed-llama

C++ distributed inference engine using custom TCP protocol. Built specifically for pipeline and tensor splitting across home PCs and heterogeneous devices.
